Critical thinking is a skill that universities expect students to develop, although it is often misunderstood as simply disagreeing with other people. Cottrell (2005) explains that critical thinking means examining the reasoning behind an idea and deciding how much trust it deserves, instead of accepting a claim because it sounds convincing or because an authority said it. One example from my own study made this clear. In a report on renewable energy I repeated a blog's claim that solar panels produce enough electricity within a year to cancel out the energy used to make them. When I checked the original sources, the payback period was much longer, so I rewrote the sentence and explained the uncertainty. That small check changed my conclusion, and it showed me that critical thinking is mostly a habit of checking rather than a special talent. A reader who follows the evidence carefully is less likely to be misled, which is why academic writing rewards patience of this kind.
